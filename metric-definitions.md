# KPI Definitions

This document defines the key performance indicators used in the Healthcare Operations KPI Dashboard.

All metrics and thresholds in this portfolio project are fictional and are intended solely to demonstrate healthcare operations analysis and performance-management concepts.

## Visit Volume

### Visits
The total number of completed patient visits during the reporting month.

**Operational significance:**  
Visit volume helps leaders evaluate patient demand, organizational capacity, provider workload, and overall practice activity.

### Visit Budget
The planned or budgeted number of patient visits for the reporting month.

**Operational significance:**  
Comparing actual visits to budget helps identify whether demand and productivity are performing above or below expectations.

---

## Patient Access

### New Patient Access Days
The approximate number of calendar days between a new patient's request for care and the next available new-patient appointment.

**Operational significance:**  
Increasing access days may indicate growing demand, insufficient provider capacity, scheduling inefficiencies, or an imbalance between appointment supply and patient demand.

**Direction:** Lower is generally better.

---

## Provider & Schedule Performance

### Provider Utilization
The percentage of available provider capacity that is being used for patient care.

**Operational significance:**  
High utilization can indicate efficient use of provider capacity but may also signal limited flexibility for additional demand.

Sustained very high utilization combined with worsening patient access can indicate a capacity constraint.

### Template Utilization
The percentage of available appointment slots that are filled or otherwise utilized.

**Operational significance:**  
Template utilization helps leaders understand how effectively scheduling capacity is being used.

High template utilization combined with worsening access may indicate that existing appointment supply is approaching capacity.

---

## Patient Communication

### Call Answer Rate
The percentage of incoming patient calls answered by the organization or designated scheduling team.

**Operational significance:**  
Call-answer performance can affect patient access, scheduling, patient experience, and administrative workload.

Declining call-answer rates may indicate staffing constraints, increased demand, inefficient workflows, or excessive call volume.

**Direction:** Higher is generally better.

---

## Patient Experience

### Patient Experience
A composite measure representing patients' overall experience with the organization.

**Operational significance:**  
Patient experience measures provide insight into whether operational processes are meeting patient expectations.

### Ease of Scheduling
The percentage of patients reporting a positive experience with the scheduling process.

**Operational significance:**  
This measure can provide an early indication of access problems, scheduling friction, appointment availability issues, or communication barriers.

**Direction:** Higher is generally better.

### Staff Worked Together
The percentage of patients reporting that members of the healthcare team worked well together.

**Operational significance:**  
This measure can reflect teamwork, communication, coordination, and consistency of the patient experience.

**Direction:** Higher is generally better.

---

## Financial Performance

### Revenue
Total fictional monthly operating revenue generated by the ambulatory practice.

**Operational significance:**  
Revenue trends can be evaluated alongside visit volume, productivity, and utilization to understand financial performance.

### Revenue Budget
The fictional planned monthly revenue target.

**Operational significance:**  
Comparing actual revenue to budget helps leaders identify positive or negative financial variance and evaluate whether operational performance is translating into expected financial results.

---

## Interpreting KPIs Together

Individual healthcare KPIs should generally not be evaluated in isolation.

For example:

- Increasing visit volume may initially indicate healthy organizational growth.
- Increasing provider and template utilization may demonstrate effective capacity use.
- However, if utilization continues increasing while new-patient access worsens, the organization may be approaching a capacity constraint.
- If call-answer performance and ease of scheduling decline at the same time, the problem may extend beyond provider capacity into administrative staffing or workflow design.

The objective of the dashboard is therefore not simply to report individual measures, but to identify relationships among operational metrics and support informed leadership decisions.
