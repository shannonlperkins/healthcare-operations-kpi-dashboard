# Healthcare Operations Performance Analysis

## Executive Summary

The synthetic ambulatory practice demonstrated positive overall financial and visit performance during the year while experiencing a significant midyear operational capacity constraint.

Annual visit volume reached **51,370 visits**, compared with a budget of **51,100**, finishing approximately **0.5% above plan**.

Annual revenue reached **$8.925 million**, compared with a budget of **$8.820 million**, finishing approximately **$105,000 or 1.2% above plan**.

However, monthly performance indicates that increasing demand placed growing pressure on provider capacity, appointment availability, and patient communication infrastructure during the middle of the year.

The most significant operational pressure occurred between June and August.

---

## Key Finding 1: Patient Demand Increased Faster Than Available Capacity

Patient visit volume increased steadily from **3,985 visits in January** to a peak of **4,515 visits in July**, representing an increase of approximately 13%.

During the same period:

* Provider utilization increased from 84% to 95%
* Template utilization increased from 88% to 95%
* New-patient access increased from 5 days to 12 days

These trends suggest that the practice successfully absorbed increasing patient demand initially, but existing provider and appointment capacity eventually became constrained.

### Leadership Interpretation

High utilization alone is not necessarily negative. Strong utilization can indicate efficient use of available resources.

However, when high utilization occurs simultaneously with worsening patient access, it can indicate that the organization has limited remaining capacity to accommodate additional demand.

The combination of approximately 95% provider utilization, 95% template utilization, and a 12-day new-patient access time suggests the practice was approaching or operating at practical capacity during July.

---

## Key Finding 2: Access Pressure Extended Beyond Provider Schedules

The capacity issue was not limited to provider appointment availability.

Call-answer performance declined from **84% in January to 72% in July**, while ease-of-scheduling performance declined from **89% to 80%**.

This occurred during the same period that visit volume and provider utilization were increasing.

### Leadership Interpretation

The simultaneous deterioration of:

* New-patient access
* Call-answer performance
* Ease of scheduling

suggests that increasing patient demand was creating pressure across the broader patient-access system.

This could indicate insufficient administrative capacity, inefficient scheduling workflows, increased call demand, or a need to redesign how patients access services.

Simply increasing provider productivity would therefore be unlikely to address the entire problem.

---

## Key Finding 3: Core Patient Experience Remained Relatively Resilient

Despite worsening access indicators, the overall patient-experience measure remained between **94% and 96%** throughout the year.

The Staff Worked Together measure also remained strong, ranging from **95% to 97%**.

### Leadership Interpretation

The stability of these measures suggests that patients continued to report a positive experience once they reached the care team, even while access to the organization became more difficult.

This distinction is operationally important.

The data suggests that the primary patient-experience risk was related to **access and scheduling infrastructure**, rather than a broad deterioration in the quality of staff interactions or team coordination.

---

## Key Finding 4: Financial Performance Remained Strong Despite Operational Strain

Revenue increased as visit volume increased and ultimately finished the year above budget.

Annual revenue totaled approximately **$8.925 million**, compared with a budget of **$8.820 million**.

This represents a favorable annual variance of approximately **$105,000**.

### Leadership Interpretation

The practice demonstrated the ability to convert growing patient demand into positive financial performance.

However, continued growth without addressing the midyear access constraints could eventually create downstream risks, including:

* Patient leakage
* Reduced new-patient growth
* Staff burnout
* Increasing scheduling complaints
* Reduced call responsiveness
* Provider capacity limitations
* Deteriorating patient experience

Short-term financial success should therefore be evaluated alongside indicators of operational sustainability.

---

## Key Finding 5: Performance Improved During the Final Quarter

Following the midyear peak in operational pressure, several indicators improved.

By December:

* New-patient access returned to 5 days
* Provider utilization decreased to 86%
* Template utilization decreased to 89%
* Call-answer performance improved to 86%
* Ease of scheduling returned to 89%
* Overall patient experience reached 96%

### Leadership Interpretation

The simultaneous improvement across provider utilization, appointment access, phone performance, and scheduling experience suggests that the system regained operational capacity during the final portion of the year.

Further analysis would be needed to determine whether this improvement resulted from:

* Seasonal demand changes
* Staffing adjustments
* Schedule-template changes
* Provider capacity changes
* Workflow improvements
* Patient-demand shifts

Leadership should determine whether the improvement represents a sustainable operational change or normal seasonal variation.

---

# Leadership Recommendations

## 1. Establish Capacity Thresholds

Create defined operational thresholds for provider and template utilization.

For example, sustained utilization above a designated level could trigger a capacity review before patient access deteriorates significantly.

The objective should be proactive capacity management rather than waiting until access becomes constrained.

## 2. Develop an Integrated Access Scorecard

Monitor access measures together rather than independently.

The scorecard should include:

* New-patient access
* Provider utilization
* Template utilization
* Call-answer performance
* Ease of scheduling
* Visit demand

This would allow leadership to identify emerging access pressure earlier.

## 3. Evaluate Patient Access Staffing

Review staffing levels and workload patterns during periods of elevated patient demand.

Analysis should include:

* Call volume by day and hour
* Scheduling demand
* Abandonment patterns
* Staffing coverage
* Administrative workload
* Appointment-request volume

This analysis could determine whether administrative capacity is aligned with patient demand.

## 4. Conduct Provider Capacity Analysis

Evaluate provider-level capacity rather than relying exclusively on practice-level averages.

Potential areas for analysis include:

* Individual provider utilization
* Appointment-template availability
* New-patient capacity
* Visit-type distribution
* Schedule-release patterns
* Cancellation and no-show rates

This can identify whether capacity constraints are organization-wide or concentrated among particular providers or services.

## 5. Create Leading Indicators

Several measures in this dataset may function as early-warning indicators.

For example, rising provider utilization and template utilization preceded the most severe deterioration in access.

Leadership could establish triggers that prompt intervention before new-patient access and scheduling experience decline.

---

# Proposed Operational Response

Based on the observed trends, the recommended approach would be to establish a recurring access and capacity review that combines patient demand, provider capacity, scheduling performance, communication metrics, and patient experience.

Rather than optimizing a single KPI, leadership should evaluate the relationship among these measures and intervene when several indicators begin moving in an unfavorable direction.

The goal is to maintain growth while protecting patient access, employee workload, provider capacity, financial performance, and the patient experience.
